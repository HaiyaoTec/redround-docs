---
title: "DNS TXT record"
description: "Verify domain ownership by adding a RedRound TXT record at your DNS provider."
---

Add a TXT record that contains your workspace's verification value. This method works for any domain, and a record on the root domain covers all its subdomains.

## Record details

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Type** | `TXT` |
| **Name** | `@` for the root domain, or the subdomain label, such as `app` for `app.example.com` |
| **Value** | `redround-verification=<value>` |
| **TTL** | Your provider's default, or 300 seconds |

The console shows the exact name and value with copy buttons.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the ownership dialog">
    In **Assets**, open the domain and select **Verify domain**. Choose where to publish the proof, then choose **DNS TXT**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the record">
    Sign in to your DNS provider and add a TXT record with the name and value shown. Keep any existing TXT records, such as SPF.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify">
    Return to RedRound and select **Verify proof**. Most providers publish records within minutes. Some take longer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check the record yourself

```bash
dig +short TXT example.com
```

The output must include a line with your exact value:

```text
"redround-verification=<value>"
```

## Notes

- Several TXT records can exist on the same name. RedRound accepts the proof if any of them matches exactly.
- If your provider adds the domain to the name automatically, enter only `@` or the subdomain label, not the full domain.
- Keep the record in place. RedRound checks it again before every pentest.
